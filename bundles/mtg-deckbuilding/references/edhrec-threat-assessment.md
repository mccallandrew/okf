# Overview

EDHREC “How To Be New” piece on **threat assessment**: identifying who will win if left alone, who to attack, and how resource engines (not life totals) drive multiplayer pressure.[^edhrec-threat]

Useful both as in-game skill and as a lens on what makes a deck feel “good” or oppressive at a table.

# Key takeaways

* Life total is a clock, not the score—high-life players with no board are often safer than low-life players with runaway engines.
* **Cards and mana** are the foundational threats; outsized free draw or acceleration marks someone as dangerous even before their wincon appears.
* Treasure and other hard-to-eyeball mana can hide threat; track them deliberately.
* Watch growth (counters, tokens), impending finishers, and **layers of protection** (Voltron hexproof/ward/indestructible)—artifact/enchantment removal is often underbuilt.
* Social play: players signal “not the threat”; assessment stays imperfect and benefits from explaining your reads.
* Construction implication: decks that reliably generate resource advantages *are* the threats others will (and should) answer first.
