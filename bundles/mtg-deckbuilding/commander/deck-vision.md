# Definition

A **deck vision** (or rough sketch) is a handful of constraints written before the 99: what the deck does, how much it can cost, how strong it should be, and whom it is built for.[^341-build]

It is coarser than a [game plan](/foundations/game-plan.md) (win lines and keepable hands) but earlier: vision picks the problem space; the plan is how you win inside it.

# Sketch checklist

Write answers, even vaguely:

1. **Theme / engine** — Graveyard? Big spells? Tokens? (Top-down fantasy or bottom-up must-includes.)
2. **Budget** — Hard ceiling and whether upgrades are expected later.
3. **Power target** — Precon tables, upgraded casual, high power, or cEDH; prefer [brackets](/commander/power-level-and-brackets.md) when discussing with others.[^wiz-brackets]
4. **Playgroup fit** — Viable without steamrolling regular opponents.
5. **Commander lock?** — “This card must be the commander” (bottom-up) vs still open (top-down discovery).

Example sketch (Guilfoyle): graveyard advantage; ~€100 with an upgrade path; cast and copy big instants/sorceries; strong enough for precon/budget pods without overshooting.[^341-build]

# Why it matters

* Filters commander candidates and package names before shopping for cards.
* Makes “pathway to power” explicit—budget shells can leave slots for later package swaps (e.g. battlecruiser payoffs → combo packages).
* Prevents mixing incompatible goals (five-color identity on a tight land budget; attraction-engine glass ceilings when you want a high power ceiling).
