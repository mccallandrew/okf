# Definition

A **game plan** is the answer to “how does this deck win?” It is more specific than an archetype label: the sequence of threats, answers, and resources you expect to deploy, and what a keepable hand looks like.

Without a plan, card choices drift toward “powerful in a vacuum” and the list fails under pressure.[^flores-threat]

# Guidelines

* State the plan in one sentence (e.g. “curve creatures into burn”; “answer everything then resolve a planeswalker”; “assemble A+B under countermagic”).
* Every maindeck card should advance the plan, enable it (mana, tutors, cantrips), or buy the time the plan needs.
* Know your **failure modes**: mana screw/flood, missing interaction, racing too slowly, or drawing the wrong half.
* Matchup plans can differ from the default—see [Who's the Beatdown](/foundations/whos-the-beatdown.md).[^flores-beatdown]

# Format notes

* **Constructed**: Plan must survive a known metagame; flex slots and sideboards encode plan B.
* **Limited**: Plan is often “good creatures on curve plus removal”; splash and bomb lines are secondary plans.
* **Commander**: The commander frequently *is* the plan; the 99 support casting it and closing games.
