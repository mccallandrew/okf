# Definition

**Card advantage** is being ahead in usable cards—drawing extras, two-for-ones, or stranding the opponent’s cards. It is the engine behind many control and midrange wins once the early race is stabilized.[^flores-beatdown]

Not all “+1 cards” are equal: a drawn land when flooding is weaker than a drawn spell when short on action.

# Guidelines

* Build advantage that fits the plan: cantrips for combo/tempo, reciprocal advantage (wraths, edicts) for control, attrition trades for midrange.
* Aggressive decks often **trade card advantage for tempo**—do not dilute the clock with slow draw unless the metagame demands it.
* Virtual card advantage matters: making the opponent’s cards dead (discard, hate) or irrelevant (racing past).
* Count resources after board wipes and multi-spell turns when evaluating “fair” midrange stacks.[^flores-threat]

# Format notes

* **Commander**: Raw card draw is mandatory in most decks; see [Category Budgets](/commander/category-budgets.md).
* **Limited**: Two-for-ones (combat tricks into removal, bombardments) decide many games.
