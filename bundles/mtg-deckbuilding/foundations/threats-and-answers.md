# Definition

**Threats** are cards that demand a response or will win if unanswered. **Answers** remove, neutralize, or race past threats. Construction is largely about how many of each you need for your plan and the expected field.[^flores-threat]

# Guidelines

* Active plans need enough threats that the opponent cannot answer everything; reactive plans need enough answers to reach [inevitability](/foundations/inevitability.md).[^flores-threat]
* Cost matters: cheap threats punish expensive answers; efficient answers punish greedy curves.
* “Answer” includes soft answers (bounce, tap, fog) and hard answers (destroy, exile, counter)—match them to what the format presents.
* Sideboards often shift threat/answer ratios after you know the opponent’s plan.[^flores-beatdown]

# Format notes

* **Limited**: Removal is scarce; overloading on answers leaves you without a clock.
* **Commander**: Multiplayer changes answer efficiency (one removal vs many threats); stack interaction and board wipes differently than duels. Threat assessment keys off **resources** (cards and mana) and runaway boards more than life totals—engines that outpace the table are the threats others will answer first.[^edhrec-threat]
