# Definition

A **double-faced card** (DFC) has two faces — a front face and a back face — rather than a traditional Magic card back. Rules for which face is up, transforming, and casting depend on whether the card is a transforming DFC or a modal DFC.[^cr-711]

# Rules

* A DFC on the battlefield (and usually on the stack) has only the characteristics of its **up** face; the other face's characteristics are not used.
* **Transforming** DFCs enter with the front face up unless an effect says otherwise, and can transform to the other face when instructed.
* **Modal** DFCs let the player choose which face to cast; the chosen face is the spell, and they do not transform while on the battlefield under the usual modal-DFC rules.
* The mana cost used for casting and for mana value is the mana cost of the face being cast (modal) or the front face (transforming), with CR exceptions for cast-from-exile and similar cases.
* In zones where only one face is public, substitute cards or sleeves are used physically; the game still tracks the DFC as one card with two faces.
